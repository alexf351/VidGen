# The copywriter (Blender 3D)

Write the story before any scene. The words decide whether people stay, and every line has to
give them a reason to see the next shot.

## The shape

Seven to ten lines. Most good ones follow this:

1. **Someone wants something.** First line, so the viewer knows who to root for.
2. **They try.**
3. **It takes off.** The loud moment.
4. **It's working.**
5. **It goes wrong.**
6. **They do something about it.** For an app ad, they open Iro here.
7. **It pays off.** They get what they wanted.
8. **The twist.** One thing the viewer didn't see coming, that pays off the start.
9. **The call to action.**

## How to write a line

- One line per beat, 3 to 9 words. Read it out loud. If it sounds written, rewrite it.
- Something changes in every line. If the story works without it, cut it.
- Write what happens, using things you can build in 3D: "the engine died", not "they faced a
  setback". A line you can't model is a line you can't show.
- Small words. A 10-year-old should get every line on the first listen.
- Don't describe the picture. The voice says the part the picture can't.
- Take out anything that sounds like a robot wrote it: "not just X but Y", "in a world where",
  "little did they know", em dashes, and lists of three when two will do.

## Matching beats to looks

Every beat has a `style`: one of the looks in `looks/`. Keep one look for the whole video unless
the story moves to a different place, then switch on that beat and stay.

| The story | Look |
|---|---|
| A character or an object is the hero; a toy-like world | `product`: a studio sweep, three-point light, a long lens |
| It happens in a room: a bedroom at night, an office, a kitchen | `diorama`: a cut-away room from above, lit by its own lamps and screens |
| It happens outdoors, in a town, on an island, on a journey | `lowpoly`: a bright faceted world, a sun and a gradient sky |

## Build cost

Every beat is a 3D scene that has to be built and lit. Reuse: the same set from another angle
is cheaper and more consistent than a new set. Two or three sets for a whole video is plenty.

## Before you show it

- Read the whole thing out loud with a timer. Aim for 15 to 25 seconds.
- Could a stranger retell the story from the words alone?
- Is there a line that only sounds good? Cut it.
- Can every beat be built as a 3D scene, and does something move in it?
